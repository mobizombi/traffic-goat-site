## Post 1 (Monday) — Affiliate Activation

Most affiliate programs don't have a traffic problem.

They have an activation problem.

I've seen programs with 200+ approved partners where fewer than 20 ever sent a single click. The program wasn't short on potential affiliates — it was short on a reason for those affiliates to actually start.

The pattern is almost always the same:
→ Onboarding ends at "approved," not at "running traffic"
→ No follow-up in the first 14 days, which is the window that actually matters
→ Same creative pack from two years ago
→ Payout structure that doesn't make the math work for a serious affiliate

You don't need more affiliates. You need the ones you already have to wake up.

## Post 2 (Wednesday) — AI and Strategy

AI should not replace strategy. It should remove operational drag.

Two ways growth teams use AI right now:

1. Asking it to make the call — what offer to lead with, which partner to prioritize, how to position. This consistently underperforms. AI doesn't know your market or your relationships.

2. Asking it to eliminate the four hours of drag that happen after the decision is made — the reporting, the first-pass outreach drafts, the call note summaries, the pre-call research.

Only one of these is where the real leverage is.

The operators getting the most out of AI right now aren't outsourcing judgment. They're buying back hours so they can use their own judgment five more times a week instead of one.

## Post 3 (Friday) — Revenue Leaks

Ask any team how their part of the funnel is doing and they'll have a clean answer.

Ask where the business is actually losing money, and most go quiet.

That's not a coincidence. The biggest revenue leaks rarely live inside one team's dashboard — they live in the seams between teams:

→ Between traffic and conversion (the campaign hit its CPC target, nobody checked if that traffic actually converts)
→ Between affiliate and product (partners are sending qualified traffic into a funnel that changed last quarter)
→ Between tools (CRM says one number, billing says another, someone reconciles it badly once a month)
→ Between acquisition and retention (everyone's incentivized to bring people in, nobody's incentivized to keep them)

The instinct when revenue underperforms is to launch something new. But if the leak is in a handoff that already exists, a new campaign just pours more traffic into the same hole.
