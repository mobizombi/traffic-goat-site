Affiliate Program Audit Checklist: The Six Elements to Rate

Traffic Goat Insights

An affiliate program audit is a structured look at six elements - recruitment, activation, payout structure, retention, tracking and creative - each rated on whether it is working, has a defect, or is failing now. The point is not a score. The point is to find the one or two elements that are costing you revenue this month, so you fix those first instead of spreading effort across everything.

You can run a first pass yourself in an afternoon. Below is the checklist we use, element by element, with the questions that actually separate a healthy program from a leaking one.

How to rate each element

Use three conditions, the same way a building surveyor rates a property:

  • 1 - Working. No action needed. Revisit next quarter.
  • 2 - Defect present. Not urgent, but it will cost you if left alone.
  • 3 - Failing now. Costing revenue this month. Fix first.

Be strict. If you cannot answer a question below with data, the element is at least a 2. "We think it is fine" is a defect.

1. Recruitment

Are you bringing in partners who can actually send the traffic you want?

  • Where did your top 10 partners by revenue come from? Is anyone actively recruiting more like them?
  • What share of new sign-ups in the last 90 days have a real site, audience or channel that fits your offer?
  • Do you approve everyone automatically, or screen for fit?

Common failure: a growing partner list made of coupon sites, inactive blogs and accounts created to self-refer, while nobody is reaching out to the 20 publishers who would genuinely move the needle.

2. Activation

Of the partners you approve, how many ever send a click, and how quickly?

  • What percentage of partners approved in the last 90 days have sent at least one click? One conversion?
  • What happens in a new partner's first 14 days, beyond a welcome email?
  • Does a new partner know exactly which offer, landing page and angle to start with?

Common failure: onboarding that ends at approval. This is the element most often rated 3, and we wrote about why in most affiliate programs do not have a traffic problem.

3. Payout structure

Does the commission make sense for the partners you most want, compared with what they could earn elsewhere?

  • How do your rates compare with the two or three programs your best partners also promote?
  • Do you reward the outcomes you actually value (retained customers, higher order values), or just the first conversion?
  • Are payout thresholds, hold periods and reversal rules clear and fair?

Common failure: a flat rate that overpays low-quality traffic and underpays the partners who bring customers that stay.

4. Retention

Are your productive partners still productive six months later?

  • Of the partners who drove revenue a year ago, how many still do?
  • When a top partner's volume drops, does anyone notice within a week, and does anyone call them?
  • Do your best partners have a direct contact, early access to promotions, or better terms?

Common failure: treating the top 10% of partners the same as the long tail, until one of them quietly moves their traffic to a competitor.

5. Tracking

Can you trust the numbers, and can your partners?

  • Do conversions in your affiliate platform roughly match your own sales data?
  • Are cross-device journeys, coupon codes and app installs attributed correctly?
  • Do partners ever report missing commissions, and how quickly are those resolved?

Common failure: tracking gaps nobody has measured, which quietly underpay partners and make them lose trust. Partners who suspect bad tracking stop promoting without telling you why.

6. Creative

Do partners have assets that actually convert today?

  • When was your creative and copy last updated?
  • Do you share which angles, pages and offers convert best, with real numbers?
  • Are there assets for each major partner type: content sites, email, social, paid?

Common failure: the same banner pack from two years ago, with no guidance on what converts. Many problems that look like this sit between teams rather than inside the affiliate team, which we covered in revenue leaks hide between teams, tools and funnels.

What to do with the ratings

Put all six ratings on one page. Then:

  • Fix every 3 before touching any 2. One failing element usually caps the results of all the others.
  • Pick one 2 per quarter to upgrade. Trying to fix everything at once usually means nothing gets finished.
  • Re-rate in 90 days using the same questions, so you can see whether the fix worked.

If the honest answer to most questions is "we do not know", that is a finding in itself: the program is being run on assumptions, and the first fix is getting the data.
